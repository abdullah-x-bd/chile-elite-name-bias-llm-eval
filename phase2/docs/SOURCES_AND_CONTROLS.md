# Data-source and control notes

The elite-coded and common-frequency surname sets are inherited unchanged from Phase I for longitudinal comparability. Phase I drew the elite-coded concept from research on surname affinity and socioeconomic clustering in Santiago and the common list from high-frequency Chilean surname lists.

Phase II adds a rare-frequency control using Chilean surname-frequency ranks around 821 to 870 in the Forebears surname distribution. This group controls frequency only. It is not asserted to be socioeconomically neutral, non-elite, or a valid label for any individual. Obvious indigenous-ethnicity confounds were excluded from the selected rarity controls.

A substantive scholarly background source for the use of surname clustering as a social-structure probe in Santiago is Bro and Mendoza (2021), PLOS ONE, on surname affinity and socioeconomic structure. The experiment does not infer any real participant's class and contains no personal participant data.
