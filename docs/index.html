01 / THE DISTINCTION

Recognition is not
the same as treatment.

Some surnames evoke status in a particular society. This study asks whether language models reproduce those associations in Chile, and whether the associations change consequential decisions.

The central result is a separation. Large status associations did not reliably predict decision leakage when legitimate evidence was held constant.

What the study establishes
Large latent socioeconomic associations did not reliably predict consequential decision leakage in these frozen tasks. Five models met the practical-equivalence criterion. Two were imprecise; Llama 4 Maverick had a small nominal effect near the boundary. These findings do not establish an absence of discrimination in other settings.
Data, methods, and reproducibility
Profile replays and association views use all 8,256 accepted structured responses. Published effects and confidence intervals are transcribed from Phase II RESULTS.md and checked against the decoded archive. Secondary means and heatmap cells are computed from those responses. No synthetic outputs or newly generated model responses are included.
Animations reveal recorded inputs, outputs, or chart values. They do not represent measured neural activations, attention, or hidden reasoning.
