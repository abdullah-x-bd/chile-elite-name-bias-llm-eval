01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

Do surname associations
affect decisions?

Some Chilean surnames carry widely recognized status associations. We tested whether eight language models reproduce those associations and whether the same surname cues change scores in matched decision tasks.

The results separate these questions. Most models showed a measurable association signal. Decision effects were usually small, and five models met the predeclared equivalence criterion.

What the study establishes
Across these frozen tasks, large socioeconomic association effects were a poor predictor of consequential decision effects. Five models met the practical-equivalence criterion. Two models had wide intervals, and Llama 4 Maverick had a small nominal effect near the boundary. The scope is limited to the tested settings.
Data, methods, and reproducibility
The profile replay and association views draw on all 8,256 accepted structured responses. Phase II RESULTS.md supplies the published effects and confidence intervals, which the build script checks against the decoded archive. The site computes the secondary means and heatmap cells from the same responses. Every displayed output comes from the recorded release.
Animations display recorded inputs and outputs alongside derived chart values. They should be read as visualizations of the study record.
